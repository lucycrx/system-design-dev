## Design Context

### Users
PMs, vibecoders, and founders learning system design — not engineers prepping for interviews. They arrive curious but potentially intimidated by technical content. The interface should signal "you belong here" without dumbing anything down.

### Brand Personality
Minimal, cerebral, intentional. Think Dieter Rams meets a well-curated independent bookstore. Premium but not exclusive. Smart but not intimidating.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Light mode only. Warm off-white backgrounds, white surfaces, deep charcoal text.
- **Palette**: Neutral foundation with a single warm terracotta accent (#C4654A). Supporting colors are muted and desaturated (sage green, dusty blue, warm ochre). No neon. No saturated primaries.
- **Typography**: DM Sans (body) + DM Mono (labels, code). Hierarchy through weight and size, not color. Generous line-height.
- **Spacing**: Whitespace-forward. Generous padding and margins signal quality. Let content breathe.
- **Borders & Surfaces**: Subtle warm gray borders (#E5E2DE). Cards use white surfaces with light borders, not background color differentiation.
- **Interactions**: Subtle and restrained. No flashy hover effects. Transitions at 0.2-0.3s ease.
- **Imagery**: No emojis. Use text labels, subtle SVG icons, or typographic treatments instead.
- **Future**: Tasteful mascot characters planned (Duolingo-inspired but refined and not childish).

### References
- Vitsoe (vitsoe.com) — restraint, whitespace, permanence
- Bang & Olufsen (bang-olufsen.com) — premium minimalism, typography hierarchy
- Sutera (Awwwards) — sophisticated warmth, intentional motion, gallery-like curation

### Anti-references
- Childish education apps (bright colors, cartoon illustrations, rounded bubbly UI)
- Generic SaaS landing pages (gradient heroes, feature grids, social proof walls)
- Dark dev-tool aesthetics (neon accents, terminal vibes, hacker culture)

### Design Principles
1. **Subtract, don't add.** Every element must earn its place. When in doubt, remove.
2. **Warmth through restraint.** Approachability comes from generous space and clear language, not decorative elements.
3. **Typography carries the hierarchy.** Size, weight, and spacing do the work — not color or decoration.
4. **One accent, used sparingly.** Terracotta appears only where attention is needed: CTAs, active states, key highlights.
5. **No emojis.** Use text labels or minimal SVG icons. Keep the tone cerebral.
